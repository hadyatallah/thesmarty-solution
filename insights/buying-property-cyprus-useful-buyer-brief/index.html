Buying property in Cyprus: how to prepare a useful buyer brief

A practical guide to defining your intended use, location, overall budget and timing before starting a property enquiry in Cyprus.

By The Smarty Solution

A useful property search starts with a clear description of what you need. Before comparing homes in Cyprus, spend a little time preparing a buyer brief. It can be one page, written in ordinary language, and updated as you learn more.

The aim is to help anyone supporting your search understand your priorities, limits and unanswered questions. You do not need every answer before making an initial enquiry.

Start with how you will use the property

Explain whether you are considering a main home, somewhere for occasional stays, or another intended use. Describe who will use it, how often, and what an ordinary day there should look like. Working from home, hosting visitors or avoiding stairs can each change which properties deserve attention.

If you have more than one possible use in mind, identify the main one. Ask appropriate professionals to assess whether any proposed use is suitable for a particular property.

Describe the location through everyday needs

Naming a town or district is a starting point. Add what matters within that area: access to work, shops, healthcare, transport or the airport; a quieter setting; or being able to walk to everyday essentials.

Separate places you already know from areas you would like to explore. If you are unfamiliar with Cyprus, say so. Time spent understanding an area can help you refine the brief before arranging a long list of viewings.

Plan for the whole commitment

Distinguish your preferred purchase-price range from your overall spending limit. Leave room to investigate buying costs, professional fees, any financing expenses, and the work or furnishings needed to make the property usable.

Also consider how ongoing maintenance and other ownership expenses would fit your plans. These are categories to discuss and verify, rather than figures to assume. Ask qualified advisers for estimates relevant to the property and your circumstances before treating a budget as complete.

Rank requirements honestly

Keep a short list of genuine must-haves, such as accessibility, minimum usable space or a firm location constraint. Put desirable features in a separate list and identify anything that would rule a property out.

This makes trade-offs easier to discuss. If every preference is treated as essential, useful options may be excluded before you have considered them properly.

Explain timing and readiness

State when you hope to view, decide and use the property. Mention dependencies, such as selling another home, arranging finance or coordinating a move. Distinguish a firm deadline from a preferred date, and be clear if you are still exploring.

Finish with the questions you need answered

Record what you want to understand about a potential property: what is included, its condition, expected running costs, and which documents your own advisers should review before you commit. Ask which licensed or qualified professional is responsible for each specialist question.

For an initial enquiry, share a concise outline. Avoid sending identity documents, bank statements or detailed financial records unnecessarily.
